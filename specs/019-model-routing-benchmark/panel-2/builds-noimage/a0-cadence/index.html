Product

Everything between "merged" and "live," handled

Cadence sits in your pipeline and makes the boring, dangerous parts of releasing software boring in the good way.

Merge queue with a heartbeat

Commits batch automatically and go out on a fixed cadence — hourly, nightly, or your own schedule — instead of whenever someone remembers to click deploy.

Canary by default

Every rollout starts at 5% and climbs on its own, watching latency and error budgets so it never has to ask you first.

Migrations, dry-run first

Schema changes replay against a shadow copy of production before they ever touch it, so the 2am surprise stays in staging.

One-key rollback

Every promoted release keeps its predecessor warm for twenty minutes. If a metric slips, cadence undo puts it back before anyone notices.

A changelog that writes itself

Every beat generates a plain-language summary of what shipped, pulled straight from commit history — ready to paste into standup.

Pipeline

Set the tempo once. Cadence keeps it.

Three steps, running on repeat, every day your team ships.

  1. 01

    Batch

    Merged PRs collect into the next release window automatically — no one has to remember to cut a release.

  2. 02

    Verify

    Migrations dry-run, canary traffic ramps, and error budgets are checked against your thresholds — all before a human is paged.

  3. 03

    Promote

    Clean signal promotes to 100% on schedule. Bad signal rolls back on its own and opens an incident with the diff attached.

FAQ

Questions release engineers actually ask

Does Cadence replace our CI?+

No. Cadence sits after CI passes — it owns batching, rollout, and promotion, not test execution.

What happens if a canary fails at 3am?+

Cadence rolls the release back automatically and posts a summary with the failing metric and the diff — no one gets paged until there's something to actually fix.

Can we set a custom release cadence?+

Yes — hourly, nightly, twice a week, or fully manual with the same safety checks still enforced.

Does it work with our existing infra?+

Cadence talks to your deploy target through a single CLI hook, so it fits in front of whatever you already run — no infra migration required.
