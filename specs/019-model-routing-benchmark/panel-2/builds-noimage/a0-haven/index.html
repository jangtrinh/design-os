Product

Everything a secret needs, nothing it doesn't

Haven does one job — hold the things that would hurt you if they leaked — and does it with almost no ceremony.

End-to-end encryption

Secrets are sealed on your device before they ever reach Haven's servers. We store ciphertext — the plaintext key never exists anywhere but your keyboard and the process that needs it.

Access that expires

Grant a secret to a person, a service, or a single deploy — for an hour or forever. Nothing is standing access by default; everything is a deliberate, revocable grant.

An audit trail you'll actually read

Every read, write, share, and rotation is recorded immutably — who, what, when, from where. When something goes wrong, you get a timeline, not a guess.

Rotation on your schedule

Set a secret to expire and Haven reminds every holder before it does, or rotates it for you and pushes the new value to every environment that's listening.

One CLI, every environment

Pull secrets into local dev, CI, and production with the same three commands. No SDK sprawl, no per-language client libraries to keep in sync.

Break-glass, done right

For the 2am incident: emergency access is possible, loud, and time-limited — a temporary hole in the wall that closes itself and tells everyone it opened.

How it works

Set up before your coffee's cold

One vault per project or per environment — your call. Everything inside inherits the same encryption and access rules.

Add people by email, add services by token. Assign roles once; Haven enforces them everywhere, every time.

03

Pull where you need it

One command hydrates your environment locally, in CI, or on a running server — no plaintext files ever touch disk.

terminal 
# authenticate once per machine
haven login

# create a vault for this project
haven vault create payments-api

# store a secret — encrypted before it leaves your shell
haven set STRIPE_SECRET_KEY --vault payments-api

# load every secret into the current environment
haven run --vault payments-api -- npm start

Security

The architecture is the pitch

Haven isn't a database with a lock icon. Every design decision starts from the assumption that our own servers could be compromised — and the vault should hold anyway.

  • Client-side sealing. Secrets are encrypted on your device with keys derived from your account — Haven's infrastructure only ever sees ciphertext.
  • Per-secret keys. No shared master key across a vault. A single compromised secret never unlocks the rest.
  • Signed, append-only audit log. Every action is hash-chained. Deleting or editing history would break the chain — and we'd notice before you did.
  • Least-privilege by default. New members and services start with nothing. Access is added, never assumed.

FAQ

Before you ask

Can Haven read my secrets?

No. Secrets are encrypted on your device before they're sent. Our servers store ciphertext and never hold the keys needed to read it back.

What happens if I lose access to my account?

Vault owners can set up recovery members ahead of time. Without a configured recovery path, a locked-out account stays locked — we designed it so we couldn't unlock it for you even if you asked.

Does Haven work with CI/CD?

Yes. The CLI authenticates via short-lived service tokens and drops straight into most CI providers with a couple of lines of config.

Can I move my existing secrets in?

Import from a `.env` file, a JSON export, or paste them in one at a time. Everything is encrypted the moment it lands.

Can I self-host?

Enterprise plans support self-hosted deployment with bring-your-own-key encryption for teams that need the vault inside their own perimeter.
