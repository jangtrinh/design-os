Three mechanisms, not a feature list.

Each one is a specific behavior a vault either does or doesn't have — not an adjective on a marketing slide.

Scoped, time-boxed leases

Every request names a scope and a duration up front — read:once, read:15m — and Haven enforces both. When the clock runs out, the value is gone; nothing lingers in an environment variable after the job that needed it has finished.

Rotation on a schedule

Long-lived credentials rotate on a timer you set, without a redeploy — the old value is revoked the moment the new one is live.

Every read, logged

Who asked, what scope, when it expired — recorded the moment a lease is granted, not reconstructed later from guesswork.

One lease, four moments.

The same ring that sits on the specimen above marks the lease at each stage — full at the request, empty the moment it's returned.

  1. 01

    Request

    A person or a service asks for a named secret, a scope, and a duration.

  2. 02

    Grant

    Haven checks the policy and issues a lease — the ring starts counting down.

  3. 03

    Use

    The value is fetched once, inside the granted scope, and never cached beyond it.

  4. 04

    Auto-expire

    The lease closes on its own. Nothing to revoke by hand, nothing left to forget about.

The habit it replaces

Teams used to paste a secret into a chat message and hope someone deleted it afterward. A lease makes that step unnecessary — it deletes itself, on time, every time, whether anyone remembers or not.
